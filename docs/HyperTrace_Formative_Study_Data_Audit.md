# Formative Study Data Audit

## Verified from the supplied workbook and PDF

- Participants: 10 anonymous IDs, `P01`--`P10`.
- Analysis material: structured written responses.
- Primary analysis units: complete responses to `Q3`--`Q6`, 40 response-level meaning units.
- `Q2`: background only; excluded from theme derivation.
- High-level themes: `F1`--`F4`.
- B-round subthemes: `T1`--`T10`, including cross-cutting `T5`, `T8`.
- Coding: two researchers independently performed AI-assisted open coding on the same anonymized material and checked their coding outputs; item-by-item comparison followed; synonymous labels were merged and granularity/theme-boundary differences were resolved by consensus.
- Documented disagreement: no contradictory conclusion-level disagreement.
- Privacy: names, contact information, IP addresses, and submission metadata were excluded from analysis.
- Participant background: approximately two years of research experience on average.
- Ethics: reviewed and approved by the IRB of the authors' college.
- Recruitment channel: intentionally not reported in the paper draft.
- Quotation bank: 12 de-identified quotations are available, with participant ID, question, quote, code, and anonymized source location.

## Four verified findings

1. Coordination judgments require mechanism-level evidence, not risk aggregation.
2. Static prediction explanations do not cover adaptive coordination.
3. Explanation claims must be traceable item by item to source records.
4. Dynamic review requires explicit and reversible explanation updates.

## Participant coverage by high-level theme

The Consensus_Coding sheet assigns one response from every participant to each
high-level theme: F1 is based on Q3, F2 on Q4, F3 on Q5, and F4 on Q6. Thus the
participant set for each theme is P01, P02, P03, P04, P05, P06, P07, P08, P09,
and P10. This is coverage of coded response units, not a frequency estimate or
evidence that every participant endorsed every subtheme.

## Evidence boundaries

- The archive does not document participant roles, professional status, region, language, compensation, collection dates, the IRB protocol number, or consent-procedure details. The authors separately confirmed the approximate mean research experience and college-IRB approval.
- The archive does not document an inter-rater reliability statistic. Do not claim Cohen's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha, saturation, or fully manual coding.
- The archive supports formative design insights, not population prevalence or a claim that all platform reviewers share these needs.
- The wording “professional reviewers” should only be used after recruitment records verify it.
- The paper should disclose AI-assisted coding and describe what researchers checked and how consensus decisions were recorded.
