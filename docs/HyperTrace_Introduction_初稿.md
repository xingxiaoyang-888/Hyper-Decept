## English Version

### 1 Introduction

Social-platform astroturfing is evolving from isolated automated accounts into coordinated account networks. Recent LLM-agent simulation studies, including persona-based engines such as DeepPersona, examine coordination under platform feedback and heterogeneous social interactions (DeepPersona, 2025; When Autonomy Goes Rogue, 2025; MADD, 2025; When AI Agents Collude Online, 2026). These controlled environments motivate a broader detection problem: an account may change its behavior over time and contribute a complementary action to a larger campaign, rather than exhibit a fixed automation signature. In such networks, an account may contribute as an agenda setter, bridge, amplifier, or peripheral executor, while the observable evidence is spread across follows, posts, replies, reposts, mentions, and time-dependent bursts. The detection target is therefore not only whether an account looks automated in isolation. It is whether a set of accounts forms a coordinated pattern whose structure and evolution are consistent with deceptive activity. This shift also changes the requirements for human review: a moderator needs to know which interactions support a decision, when those interactions became available, and whether the displayed evidence describes the coordination mechanism rather than merely correlating with the detector output.

The representation problem is inherently heterogeneous and hierarchical. A coordination graph may contain users, posts, and other content objects connected by typed relations with different directions, frequencies, and temporal meanings. Role specialization can produce a core–periphery or multi-level organization, while diffusion creates paths and bursts that cannot be summarized reliably by one static neighborhood. Recent multi-relational bot-detection benchmarks and dynamic graph models make these relation types explicit (MGTAB, 2023; BotDGT, 2024). Hyperbolic representation learning is a candidate for settings in which the observed organization is tree-like or strongly hierarchical, and recent work has developed efficient hyperbolic Transformer operators and hyperbolic heterogeneous backbones (Hypformer, 2024; HypHGT, 2026). These models address how to represent and predict over a structured graph. They do not, by themselves, specify how an explanation should preserve the manifold geometry, radial ordering, relation semantics, or the temporal boundary used by the detector. The separation matters: an encoder can use a hierarchy-aware representation while an explanation module selects evidence using an objective that is unaware of that hierarchy.

Graph explanation methods expose this gap. Foundational mask explainers define the prediction-preservation and sparsity objective (Ying et al., 2019; Luo et al., 2020), while recent work extends graph explanations to temporal motifs, diffusion-based in-distribution masks, adversarial settings, dynamic snapshots, and global rule-like summaries (TempME, 2023; D4Explainer, 2023; Adversarial Mask Explainer, 2024; DyExplainer, 2025; GNNXemplar, 2025). These objectives provide useful predictive accounts, but prediction agreement is not equivalent to structural correctness. A mask can preserve a logit while changing the geodesic distances, center–periphery ordering, or relation pattern that gives a coordination graph its meaning. Likewise, a selected event may be predictive in hindsight but unavailable at the decision cutoff, and an explanation claim without a source record cannot be independently audited. The resulting methodological question is not simply whether an explanation is faithful to a prediction, but whether it is simultaneously faithful to the geometry, roles, time boundary, and evidence provenance of the detection process.

This question has a direct human-computer interaction consequence. Content moderation is a consequential decision task in which both missed deception and unjustified intervention carry costs. Explanations can help reviewers identify a model error, but they can also make an incorrect recommendation appear more credible. Recent human-AI decision studies show that explanations and interface interventions can reduce or increase over-reliance depending on the information presented and the decision context (Vasconcelos et al., 2023; Schemmer et al., 2023; Morrison et al., 2024; de Jong et al., 2025). We therefore treat appropriate reliance—not undifferentiated trust or satisfaction—as the human outcome of interest. A useful explanation should help a reviewer accept a correct recommendation, reject an incorrect one, and understand the evidential basis for either choice. This requires the interface to expose uncertainty, temporal scope, and source records, while preserving a meaningful path for independent review.

We present **HyperTrace**, an auditable explanation workflow for coordinated-deception detection. HyperTrace formulates explanation as constrained evidence reconstruction. Given a detector output and the observations available up to a decision cutoff, the explainer searches for a compact account that preserves the prediction while satisfying four contracts: (i) **prediction fidelity**, agreement with the detector under the selected evidence; (ii) **geometry fidelity**, preservation of the Lorentz-space distances and radial organization used to represent coordination; (iii) **role fidelity**, consistency between selected relations and the interaction roles or relation types they are intended to support; and (iv) **evidence traceability**, a verifiable link from each explanation claim to a source record, timestamp, data/model version, and cutoff. A temporal state mechanism prevents future events from entering a current explanation and records state transitions as evidence becomes available. The resulting explanation packet presents uncertainty, counterfactual changes, structural evidence, and provenance together, so that the explanation can be inspected rather than accepted as an opaque score.

We evaluate HyperTrace across complementary data regimes. TwiBot-22 provides real-world account labels, profiles, posts, and social relations for static and cross-domain detection and explanation tests (Feng et al., 2022). MGTAB provides a recent external multi-relation tensor benchmark for testing relation handling and out-of-source behavior (MGTAB, 2023); its missing raw timestamps and user-level evidence limit the temporal and provenance claims that can be made from that dataset alone. Audited synthetic episodes provide controlled trajectories with privileged role, campaign-phase, and next-action annotations for mechanism-oriented tests of temporal validity and explanation state transitions. These synthetic annotations are used as controlled supervision, not as psychological diagnoses or ground truth about real platform users. The experimental protocol compares Euclidean and hyperbolic detector backbones, prediction-oriented and geometry-constrained explainers, and moderator conditions ranging from no AI and score-only assistance to HyperTrace. It measures detection performance, the four explanation dimensions, cutoff leakage and provenance coverage, latency and stability, and human error-adjusted accuracy and appropriate reliance.

This work makes three contributions:

1. **A four-dimensional explanation contract for coordinated deception graphs.** We define measurable prediction, geometry, role, and evidence requirements, making explicit what is—and is not—preserved when a mask is considered faithful.
2. **HyperTrace, a constrained evidence-reconstruction workflow.** The workflow connects an intrinsic Lorentz heterogeneous representation to geometry- and role-aware masking, cutoff-controlled temporal evidence, provenance-linked `ExplanationPacket`s, and an interface designed for independent moderator review. The contribution is the explanation contract and its auditable realization, rather than a claim to introduce a generic hyperbolic HGT backbone.
3. **A joint model-and-human evaluation protocol.** We combine real and controlled data, cross-domain and leave-one-scenario-out tests, explanation audits, and a moderator study that measures correct acceptance and wrong-recommendation rejection instead of treating increased trust as success.

Together, these contributions frame graph explanation for coordinated deception as a problem of preserving the meaning of evidence across representation, time, and human decision-making. The central research questions are whether geometry-aware constraints improve structural and role fidelity without sacrificing prediction fidelity, whether cutoff and provenance contracts make explanations more auditable, and whether these properties support better-calibrated moderator decisions.

## 中文对照版本

### 1 引言

社交平台水军活动正在从孤立的自动化账号演变为具有协同关系的账号网络。近年的大语言模型智能体仿真研究（包括 DeepPersona 等人格引擎）开始考察平台反馈下的协同以及异构社交关系中的互动（DeepPersona, 2025；When Autonomy Goes Rogue, 2025；MADD, 2025；When AI Agents Collude Online, 2026）。这些受控环境提示了一个更一般的检测问题：账号可能随时间改变行为，并为更大的 campaign 执行互补动作，而不是始终表现出固定的自动化特征。在这类网络中，不同账号可能承担议程设置、跨社区连接、内容放大或外围执行等互补功能，相关证据则分散在关注、发帖、回复、转发、提及以及随时间变化的活动突发中。因此，检测目标不再只是判断某个账号是否孤立地表现出自动化特征，而是判断一组账号是否形成了在结构和演化过程上都与欺骗活动一致的协同模式。这一变化也提高了人工审核的要求：审核员需要知道哪些交互支持了检测结论、这些交互在何时已经可用，以及解释是否描述了协同机制，而不只是复述与模型输出相关的特征。
这一表示问题同时具有异构性、层级性和时间性。协同图可以包含用户、帖子和其他内容对象，并由具有不同方向、频率和时间含义的关系连接。角色分工可能形成核心—外围结构或多层组织，而传播过程则产生无法由单一静态邻域可靠概括的路径和活动突发。近年的多关系水军检测基准和动态图模型开始显式处理这些关系类型（MGTAB, 2023；BotDGT, 2024）。当观察到的组织具有树状或明显层级结构时，双曲表示学习也提供了合适的表示空间；近期研究进一步发展了高效双曲 Transformer 算子和双曲异构骨干模型（Hypformer, 2024；HypHGT, 2026）。这些模型解决的是如何在结构化图上进行表示和预测，但它们并不会自动规定解释应当如何保留流形几何、径向层级、关系语义或检测器使用的时间边界。两者之间的分离很重要：编码器可以使用层级感知的表示，而解释模块仍可能用一个不感知该层级结构的目标来选择证据。

图解释方法正是在这里出现了方法缺口。奠基性的掩码解释方法定义了保持预测和促进稀疏性的目标（Ying et al., 2019；Luo et al., 2020），而近年的工作将图解释扩展到时序 motif、基于扩散的分布内掩码、对抗场景、动态图快照以及全局规则式总结（TempME, 2023；D4Explainer, 2023；Adversarial Mask Explainer, 2024；DyExplainer, 2025；GNNXemplar, 2025）。这些目标能够提供有用的预测层面说明，但预测一致性并不等同于结构正确性。一个掩码可能保持模型 logit 不变，却改变协同图所依赖的测地线距离、中心—外围排序或关系模式。同样，一个事件可能在事后看来具有预测性，却在决策截断时刻尚不可用；如果解释主张没有对应的来源记录，也无法被独立复核。因此，关键问题不只是解释是否忠实于预测，而是它能否同时忠实于检测过程中的几何结构、交互角色、时间边界和证据来源。

这一问题直接影响人机交互。内容审核属于具有现实后果的决策任务，漏检欺骗行为和无依据地采取干预都会产生代价。解释可能帮助审核员发现模型错误，也可能使错误建议显得更加可信。近年的人机决策研究表明，解释和界面干预对过度依赖的影响取决于所呈现的信息以及具体决策情境，既可能减少也可能增加过度依赖（Vasconcelos et al., 2023；Schemmer et al., 2023；Morrison et al., 2024；de Jong et al., 2025）。因此，我们关注的是适当依赖，而不是没有区分的信任或满意度。一个有用的解释应当帮助审核员在 AI 正确时接受建议、在 AI 错误时拒绝建议，并理解两种决定背后的证据基础。这要求界面同时呈现不确定性、时间范围和来源记录，并保留独立复核的有效路径。

我们提出 **HyperTrace**，一套面向协同欺骗检测的可审计解释工作流。HyperTrace 将解释表述为受约束的证据重构问题。给定检测器输出以及决策截断时刻之前可观察到的信息，解释器寻找一个紧凑的证据说明，使其在保持预测的同时满足四项约束：第一，**预测保真度**，即选定证据下的解释与检测器输出一致；第二，**几何保真度**，即保留用于表示协同结构的 Lorentz 空间距离和径向组织；第三，**角色保真度**，即被选中的关系与其所支持的交互角色或关系类型一致；第四，**证据可追溯性**，即每一条解释主张都能链接到可验证的来源记录、时间戳、数据/模型版本和决策截断点。时间状态机制阻止未来事件进入当前解释，并在新证据出现时记录状态转移。最终的解释包将不确定性、反事实变化、结构证据和来源信息放在同一上下文中，使解释能够被检查，而不是被当作不可审计的风险分数直接接受。

我们在互补的数据条件下评估 HyperTrace。TwiBot-22 提供真实账号标签、用户资料、帖子和社交关系，用于静态检测、跨域检测和解释测试（Feng et al., 2022）。MGTAB 提供较新的外部多关系张量基准，可用于测试关系处理能力和跨来源行为（MGTAB, 2023）；但由于缺少原始时间戳和用户级证据，不能单独支撑关于时间演化和证据来源的完整结论。经过审计的合成事件则提供带有特权角色、campaign 阶段和 next-action 标注的受控轨迹，用于机制层面的时间有效性和解释状态转移测试。这些合成标注仅作为受控监督信号使用，不被解释为真实用户的心理诊断或真实平台标签。实验协议比较欧式和双曲检测骨干、以预测为中心的解释器和几何约束解释器，并比较无 AI、仅风险分数辅助、传统解释和 HyperTrace 等审核条件。评估指标包括检测性能、四维解释质量、截断泄漏和来源覆盖率、延迟与稳定性，以及审核员的错误校正准确率和适当依赖。

本文有三项主要贡献：

1. **面向协同欺骗图的四维解释契约。** 我们定义可计算的预测、几何、角色和证据要求，明确一个掩码被称为忠实时究竟保留了哪些含义。
2. **HyperTrace 受约束的证据重构工作流。** 该工作流将 intrinsic Lorentz 异构表示、几何与角色感知掩码、受截断控制的时间证据、带来源链接的 `ExplanationPacket` 和支持独立复核的审核员界面连接起来。本文的贡献是解释契约及其可审计实现，而不是声称提出一个通用的双曲 HGT 骨干。
3. **联合模型与人因的评估协议。** 我们结合真实数据与受控数据、跨域和留一场景测试、解释审计以及审核员研究，测量正确接受和错误建议拒绝，而不是把信任增加本身当作成功标准。

总体而言，本文将协同欺骗场景中的图解释重新表述为一个跨越表示、时间和人类决策的证据语义保持问题。核心研究问题是：几何约束能否在不牺牲预测保真度的情况下提高结构和角色保真度；时间截断与来源契约能否使解释更易审计；以及这些性质能否支持审核员做出更加校准的决策。
